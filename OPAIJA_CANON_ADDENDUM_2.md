## The Five-Part Opaija System — Expanded Draft

### 1. Bois Memory (The Stick Remembers)

**Canon rule (existing):** "The stick stores ancestral combat knowledge. Older sticks = deeper memory. A stick chooses its fighter."

**Expanded mechanics:**
- A bois's memory is activated through touch + rhythm sync. A fighter cannot just grab a stick and fight — they must let the bois "recognize" them.
- When a bois activates, its carvings glow (rhythm glyphs along the shaft). The older the bois, the deeper the glow and the more combat knowledge it can channel.
- A bois can be **severed** from its fighter (Marius Vale's Silence Pulse does this). When severed, the carvings go dark and the stick becomes ordinary wood again.
- A bois that has been severed can be re-awakened, but only through the full five-part alignment again.
- **The Listening Bois (Kai's weapon):** One of the oldest surviving Opaija Seeds. It "hears" rhythm in all forms — drumbeats, heartbeats, street noise, lavway chants. This is why it chose Kai, who has natural rhythm intuition.

**Visual language for agents:**
- Glowing carvings = bois is active. Dark carvings = bois is dormant/severed.
- The glow color matches the fighter's Inner Pulse (see below).
- Kai's Listening Bois glows amber-gold (#E4A700) when active.

### 2. Inner Pulse (The Fighter's Own Rhythm)

**Canon rule (existing):** "The fighter's own spiritual rhythm — their unique beat. Must be discovered, not taught."

**Expanded mechanics:**
- Every person has an Inner Pulse, but most people never discover it. It's not a skill — it's a recognition.
- Inner Pulse manifests as a specific tempo/feel. Kai's is "Tempo Pulse" — steady, adaptable, syncs with any external rhythm. Malik's is "Root Pulse" — heavy, grounded, disciplined.
- A fighter's Inner Pulse can be disrupted by doubt, fear, or Silence Pulse. When disrupted, their bois won't activate even if all other conditions are met.
- **Discovery moment:** A fighter discovers their Inner Pulse through a moment of genuine emotional truth, not through training. Kai discovered his during the Carnival night (EP001) when the Listening Bois called his name.
- **Power scaling:** Inner Pulse grows stronger with emotional conviction, not physical training. This is why Malik (technically superior) can lose to Kai (spiritually aligned) — the bois amplifies conviction, not muscle.

**Visual language for agents:**
- Inner Pulse visualizes as a circular drumbeat ring around the fighter's feet/torso when active.
- Each fighter's ring has a unique color/pattern matching their pulse type.
- When disrupted (doubt/Silence Pulse), the ring flickers and breaks.

### 3. Lavway Command (The Chant That Activates)

**Canon rule (existing):** "Call-and-response chants that activate specific abilities. Must be sung correctly — intent matters."

**Expanded mechanics:**
- Lavway is NOT magic words. It's a call-and-response between the chanter and the bois. The bois "responds" by activating a specific combat ability.
- The chanter (Nia's role) must MEAN the words. A chanter who doesn't believe the lavway produces nothing. Intent is the activation key, not pronunciation.
- Each lavway has a specific effect:
  - **"Awake the wood"** — activates a dormant bois
  - **"Hear the fighter"** — syncs the bois to a new fighter's Inner Pulse
  - **"Break the silence"** — counters Silence Pulse effects (only works if the chanter is not afraid)
  - **"Remember the blood"** — channels ancestral combat memory from the bois
- **Lavway can be weaponized by the villain:** Marius Vale's One Drum broadcasts a corrupted lavway that suppresses all other chants. This is why Silence Pulse is so dangerous — it's not silence, it's a corrupted lavway that drowns out all others.
- **Singing vs. speaking vs. humming:** The canon rule is absolute — must be sung. Humming produces a weak, unstable activation. Speaking produces nothing. Singing with intent produces full activation. This is a visual/audio cue agents must respect.

**Visual language for agents:**
- When lavway activates, rhythm glyphs appear in the air around the chanter and travel toward the fighter's bois.
- Corrupted lavway (Marius) produces silence cracks — black fractures in the visual field instead of glyphs.
- The lavway glyphs are warm-colored (gold, amber, red) for OPAIJA. Cold-colored (silver, purple, black) for Silence Pulse.

### 4. Drum Sync (The Amplifier)

**Canon rule (existing):** "Alignment with a drummer partner amplifies all powers. Jabs is Kai's drum sync partner."

**Expanded mechanics:**
- Drum Sync is NOT just background music. The drummer must match the fighter's Inner Pulse tempo. If the drummer is off-beat, the sync fails and the fighter's power actually decreases.
- Drum Sync doubles (2x) the fighter's power output. Without a drummer, a fighter is at 50% capacity.
- **Physical presence requirement:** The drummer must be physically present or within earshot. Remote drumming (e.g., over speakers) does NOT work — the bois responds to the acoustic wave of real drums, not reproduced sound. This is a hard rule.
- Drum Sync can be disrupted by Silence Pulse targeting the drummer. If the drummer is silenced, the fighter loses their 2x amplification. This is why protecting Jabs is tactically critical.
- **Jabari's drums:** African style wooden Kalinda drums with L-shaped curved sticks. NOT a modern drum kit. NOT Western drumsticks. This is a style lock — Seedance prompts must always specify "African style wooden drums, L-shaped curved sticks."

**Visual language for agents:**
- When Drum Sync is active, the fighter's drumbeat ring doubles in intensity and the drummer's ring syncs with it.
- When sync is broken (drummer silenced), both rings fade.
- Drumbeat rings from the drummer and fighter should visibly pulse in time together.

### 5. Gayelle Vow (The Binding)

**Canon rule (existing):** "A sacred oath taken in the gayelle circle. Binds fighter to their bois permanently. Cannot be undone."

**Expanded mechanics:**
- The Gayelle Vow is the permanent lock. Before the vow, a fighter can lose their bois (Silence Pulse can sever the connection). After the vow, the connection is permanent — even Silence Pulse can suppress it but cannot sever it.
- The vow is taken inside a gayelle (the stick-fighting circle). The fighter must step into the circle with their bois and state their intention out loud. The bois responds by permanently bonding.
- **The cost:** The Gayelle Vow is irreversible. A fighter who takes the vow is bound to that specific bois forever. If the bois is destroyed, the fighter loses their Opaija power permanently. This is the stakes.
- **Not every fighter takes the vow.** Some fighters use their bois for years without taking it. The vow is a commitment — a declaration that this is who you are. Kai's vow moment is a season-defining event (not EP001 — this is a mid-season or end-of-season beat).
- **Marius Vale took a corrupted vow:** His Gayelle Vow was to the One Drum, not a bois. This is why his power is permanent but twisted — the vow mechanism works on any Opaija Seed, including corrupted ones.

**Visual language for agents:**
- After the Gayelle Vow, the fighter's bois carvings glow permanently (even when not in combat). This is a visual marker of a vowed fighter.
- The gayelle circle itself glows when a vow is taken — a ring of light on the ground.
- Marius's corrupted vow produces a black ring instead of a light ring.
