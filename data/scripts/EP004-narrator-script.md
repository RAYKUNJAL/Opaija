# Narrator script (The Web-Teller)

Memory doesn't need a language. It needs a voice.

In Nia's family yard, the evening sat down soft. Lanterns. Pothounds. Old wood remembering the day.

Nia opened her mouth and let the first lavway out — small, careful, true.

Kai listened. Kai tried. But his Inner Pulse stuttered like a foot missing a step. The chant slipped through his fingers.

"Doh sing it unless yuh mean it," she told him.

Kai bit into a doubles. Mother Lall's curry. Soft bara, slow heat. And somewhere between the channa and the pepper, the rhythm sat down inside him.

Then Jabari lifted his sticks. Nobody asked him. He just heard it. African wood. L-shaped strike. The drum answered the lavway like family answers a name.

The chant completed itself. Kai's pulse caught the beat. For one breath, three children became one rhythm.

Far across the city, something cold turned its head. A ripple. A hush. The sound of listening that does not love.
