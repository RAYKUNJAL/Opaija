# OPAIJA Action Choreography Training Plan

## Decision

Yes, the action agent can be trained as a choreography system. It should not scrape copyrighted action movies or copy protected fight scenes. Instead, build a lawful reference and rule library:

- Public-domain or licensed martial arts references
- Self-recorded staff drills and actor/stunt videos
- Technique taxonomy from kung fu staff/pole fighting, bo staff, and Trinidad bois/Kalenda movement
- Shot grammar from general filmmaking principles
- Approved Opaija action frames and Seedance outputs

## Agent Role

Action Choreographer owns:

- staff/pole technique vocabulary
- pose transitions
- attack/defense beats
- camera grammar
- impact timing
- safety/readability checks
- Seedance action prompts
- Remotion edit notes

## Movement DNA

Opaija action should combine:

- Kalenda/bois rhythm, footwork, call-and-response energy
- Kung fu staff/pole flow, circular strikes, sweeps, blocks, thrusts, and spins
- Anime timing: anticipation, smear/impact, hold, recovery, reaction
- Caribbean environmental motion: dust, cloth, drum pulses, sea wind, market paper, leaves

## Choreography Packet

Every fight scene should include:

- Scene objective
- Emotional state
- Characters involved
- Terrain/environment
- Technique list
- Beat-by-beat choreography
- Camera plan
- FX layer
- Seedance prompt
- Remotion edit notes
- Continuity and safety notes

## Hero Scene Direction

The homepage hero loop should be short and cinematic:

1. Kai runs through a Caribbean twilight scene.
2. He passes between rhythm portals/world layers.
3. He performs a staff kata inspired by pole-fighting principles.
4. He strikes through the OPAIJA name.
5. Letters fracture into gold/teal/orange shards.
6. Drumwave rings and lightning pulse outward.
7. The scene resolves into the founder-list call to action.

## Legal Guardrail

Do not copy specific fights, choreography, camera cuts, or scenes from copyrighted movies. Use them only as high-level inspiration if lawfully reviewed, then create original Opaija movement.
